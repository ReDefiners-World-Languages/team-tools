What We Learned

1

Enrollment held steady but plateaued. 55 qualified enrollments beat the 50-student goal (110%) for a fourth straight year, but settled 3.5% below 2025's peak of 57.

2

Nearly 42% of registration attempts failed at payment. 39 of 94 CRM records never converted to a paid seat — the single biggest funnel-health finding this year.

3

Registration comes in two concentrated waves. March and May are 79% of the year's registration volume — and May carries the largest share of payment failures.

4

This campaign runs on organic and relationship channels, not paid. Zero 2026 Meta spend and 84.5% of tagged website sessions arriving via email.

5

Outreach tactics do two different jobs, org-wide. Flyer Drop-off builds reach; Community Events build the relationships that convert — the same split the MCP recap found.

6

Parent satisfaction is the strongest asset. 100% satisfied overall, 100% would recommend — a strong word-of-mouth foundation for organic growth next year.

4

Recommendations for 2027

Continue

  • The email-led, multi-touch nurture sequence — the single largest driver of website traffic and, plausibly, enrollment.
  • The community outreach model concentrated at trusted partner sites, with Community Events weighted at least as heavily as Flyer Drop-off.
  • Surveying parents post-program — the satisfaction and recommend-intent data is strong and worth protecting as a recurring measurement.

Start

  • Fix the registration-payment funnel and investigate whether price, the income-based discount, or scheduling are contributing.
  • Concentrate registration reminders and nurture-email intensity specifically around the March and May windows.
  • Decide deliberately whether Summer STEAM should get any 2027 paid media allocation, rather than defaulting to organic-only again.
  • Track Chinese-language and second-location interest with a direct 2027 survey question.

Reconsider

  • Reporting Community-Based Outreach as a single blended conversations-per-flyer rate — it masks a 150x difference between tactics.
  • Treating payment failures as a marketing problem alone — schedule, programming, and pricing may be contributing to buyer hesitation too.
