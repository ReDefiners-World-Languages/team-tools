What We Learned

1

Course naming works. The renamed ESOL courses show a measurable, lasting shift in enrollment distribution — not just a perception change.

2

Growth was concentrated, not universal. Professionals, Workshops for Adults, and Spanish Youth Club drove nearly all of the net gain.

3

Flyers and conversations are two different jobs. Drop-offs build reach; tables and community events build the relationships that convert.

4

Email is the standout low-cost channel — volume nearly doubled without diluting engagement, and clicks/replies grew faster than opens.

5

LinkedIn punches above its weight — a small audience generated the highest click-per-post rate of any network, worth pairing with Professionals promotion.

6

The team can redirect paid media quickly when it matters — proven by the mid-campaign ESOL Courses push to help close the CBHC data gap.

4

Recommendations for FY26-27

Continue

  • Course renaming strategy — apply the same benefit-led naming principle to other programs with confusing names.
  • Email program investment — the highest-engagement, lowest-cost channel in this report.
  • CBFRC / Boys & Girls Club partner network for outreach — highest flyer volume, strongest partner trust.

Start

  • Track "meaningful conversations" as outreach's primary KPI, with flyers distributed as a secondary reach metric.
  • Investigate ESOL Courses and Let's Learn Courses softness directly with program staff.
  • Pair LinkedIn promotion deliberately with Professionals program enrollment windows.

Reconsider

  • Treating Flyer Drop-off as an outreach success metric on its own — reframe as reach, not conversion.
  • Decide in advance which programs get priority paid support by default, vs. a flexible reserve for mid-year corrections.
